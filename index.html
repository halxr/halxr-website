The problem

One class, one pace, every student somewhere else.

A teacher can teach at only one level and one pace. Students who start behind can't follow, and students who are ahead learn less than they could. Teaching to the middle costs learning at both ends.

Starting behind isn't the same as learning slowly. A capable student who begins in the wrong place keeps falling further behind, because every lesson builds on the one they missed.

Behind

40% of US 4th graders read below the NAEP Basic level for their grade, the most since 2002. In 8th grade math it's nearly 40%. Nation's Report Card (NAEP), 2024 ↗

Ahead

Up to 49% of US elementary and middle school students already score a year or more above grade level in reading (20–49% across four data sets; 14–37% in math). Peters et al., Gifted Child Quarterly, 2017 ↗

Further apart

~100 points between the lowest- and highest-performing students on NAEP, and the gap is widening: the lowest scores have fallen while the highest held steady. Nation's Report Card (NAEP), 2024 ↗

Both groups sit in the same classrooms, taught one lesson at one pace.

Why HAL

AI tutoring is free now. A curriculum built for one student isn't.

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Khan Academy all launched free AI tutoring in 2025. They personalize the conversation. The few products that personalize the path itself need a tablet in a learning center or a seat in a private school.

AI chat tutors

Help in the moment

Free from every major AI company. They explain well when asked, but they don't keep a model of the learner or change the course.

Adaptive learning centers

Adjust the practice

Track mastery skill by skill. The leading one beat expert-taught instruction in a randomized math trial, but runs on tablets in its own centers, starting with young children.

Mastery-based schools

Move at the student's pace

About two hours a day in adaptive apps, with adult guides in the room. Tuition runs $40,000 to $75,000 a year.

How it works

Teaching, steered like a control system.

Control engineers steer systems they can only measure imperfectly: compare where it is with where it should be, act, and measure again. HAL teaches the same way, one student at a time.

  1. Measure

    Answers, spoken explanations, response times and what the student does in VR.

  2. Estimate

    What the student knows, what they're likely to forget, and how the session is going.

  3. Decide

    Teach something new, review, check, or take a break, aiming at the right level of challenge.

  4. Teach

    The agentic teacher delivers the next lesson, then the loop runs again.

Real time

Lesson loop

Material→Student→Evaluation→Learner model→Next material

Every answer updates what HAL knows about the student, and the next step changes on the spot.

Periodic

Course loop

Learner model + new material→Course optimization→This student's curriculum

Over weeks, HAL rebuilds the path through the course around how this student actually learns.

Both loops serve one student. No one else's curriculum changes.

Research

Built on what already works in learning research.

Each part of HAL's loop draws on randomized trials of teaching at the right level, personalized review and AI tutoring, including the ways AI help can backfire.

Teach at the student's level

0.37 SD higher math scores, a large effect, after about 4.5 months of a program that taught each student at their own level instead of their grade's. HAL sets the level and pace per student. Muralidharan et al., American Economic Review, 2019 ↗

Review what each student needs

+16.5% retention a month after the course when a memory model chose what each student reviewed, and 10% over a fixed review schedule. HAL schedules review for each student. Lindsey et al., Psychological Science, 2014 ↗

Design the AI to teach

2× the learning gains of an in-class active-learning lesson, in less time, from an AI tutor built on teaching best practices. HAL's agentic teacher scaffolds and lets students set the pace. Kestin et al., Scientific Reports, 2025 ↗

Hints, not answers

−17% lower scores once unguarded AI help was taken away. A version that gave hints instead of answers largely avoided the harm. HAL's agentic teacher guides; it doesn't hand over answers. Bastani et al., PNAS, 2025 ↗
  • Measures learning, not screen time
  • Mastery checked without help, a day and a week later
  • Break prompts in every VR session
  • Students see why each lesson was chosen

The signal

A record of what you can do, owned by you.

AI can write any cover letter, and a GPA means something different at every school. HAL sees how students actually learn: how fast they master new material, what they still remember a week later, how they handle hard problems and explain their thinking.

Students see their own progress in plain terms. Over time it becomes a capability profile they own and can choose to share with employers, universities and programs that need a better signal.
